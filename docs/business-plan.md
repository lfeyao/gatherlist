# First business experiment

## The customer and task

Start with San Diego hosts organizing dinners, small founder gatherings, salons or off-sites for roughly 12–40 guests. The task is to turn scattered venue/vendor information into a defensible shortlist with sources and a clear list of questions still to answer. This segment is a hypothesis to validate, not an established customer base.

## Offer to test

A personally reviewed shortlist of three venue options for one specific brief, with source links and quote/availability questions. Use the current tool to track and compare the options. Obtain real quotes before presenting totals as confirmed. Discuss a fixed sourcing fee with potential customers after the value is clear; no price or success fee is assumed validated yet.

## First week

1. Interview five local hosts or organizers about their last event: what was hardest, how long it took, who did the work, and what made them trust a recommendation. No outreach has been sent.
2. Gather 15–20 supplier listings from official pages, supplier-provided details or notes the host is authorized to use. Record the precise room and distinguish hire fees from food/beverage minimums.
3. Pilot one real brief. Record the research time, missing fields, number of suppliers contacted by the human, usable options and why the host chose or rejected each.
4. Deliver a reviewed shortlist, ask what was valuable and whether the host would pay for another. Measure paid demand before adding a subscription or automated concierge.
5. Publish a short build walkthrough with the code and results that can be disclosed. Distinguish demo data from actual outcomes.

## Expansion after evidence

Track quote requests, replies and quote validity dates; separate venue hire, food, tax, service fees and staffing; add shared server-side records; add a consent-based supplier intake form. Introduce a model-backed extractor only with source-grounded fields and explicit review. Add integrations after a repeated workflow justifies them.

The current application supports the list and comparison stages of the referenced approach. Distribution, supplier verification, customer trust and repeat demand are still work to do.
